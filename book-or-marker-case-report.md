# Case Report: Book or Marker

**Type:** Moral thought experiment (market perspective)
**Subject:** Incentive design under existential stakes
**Prepared:** November 2, 2026

---

## 1. Executive Summary

A giant python, interrupted mid-book, is offered two "marker" bills. The $1M bill marks where it resumes reading. The $5M bill marks the end of the book. Failing to resume means all its prey are eaten. Finishing the book means the entire realm, including the python, is reset as though it never existed.

Viewed as a market, the arrangement pays the most for the most destructive outcome. This report analyzes the case, tests it under several moral frameworks, examines what happens when the bills are removed in different orders, and concludes that the situation is unavoidably a moral choice.

**Key finding:** The best-supported course is to remove the $5M end marker, keep the $1M start marker, and let the python read at its own pace. This protects the prey and removes the incentive to erase the realm, at the cost of some pressure on the python's autonomy.

---

## 2. Case Facts

| Item | Description |
|---|---|
| Actor | A giant python, interrupted while reading a book |
| Gift A | $1M "memo bill" used as a **start marker** (resume reading) |
| Gift B | $5M "memo bill" used as an **end marker** (finish reading) |
| Consequence of not resuming | All the python's prey will be eaten |
| Consequence of finishing | The entire realm is reset as if it never existed, python included |
| Decision-maker | The market, which prices and issues the markers |

### Assumptions (interpretive)

The original wording was ambiguous, so this report assumes:

- "Starter" means the point at which the python resumes reading.
- "End of reading" means completing the book.
- Reading keeps the python occupied and therefore not hunting.
- The python responds to incentives, though it may also hold its own values.

---

## 3. Stakeholders

| Stakeholder | Interest | Voice in the market |
|---|---|---|
| The python | Continue or end its story; receive payment | Full (it chooses) |
| The prey | Survival | None |
| The wider realm | Continued existence | None |
| The market | Pricing outcomes; avoiding responsibility for harm | Controls the prices |

**Observation:** The two groups with the most to lose, the prey and the realm, are the two with no bargaining power.

---

## 4. Market Analysis

### 4.1 What the prices signal

- The **$1M** price attaches to *continuation*, an outcome that keeps the prey alive.
- The **$5M** price attaches to *termination*, an outcome that erases everything.
- The 5:1 price ratio means the market pays five times more for annihilation than for preservation.

### 4.2 Externalities

- Prey being eaten is a **negative externality** of not resuming: a harm borne by parties outside the transaction.
- Realm erasure is a **total externality**. Nobody remains to register the loss.

### 4.3 Limits of price as a moral measure

- Prices reflect the python's incentive, not anyone's welfare.
- A market optimizing for the python's payout would choose the $5M outcome, the worst outcome for everyone else.

---

## 5. Scenario Analysis: Removing the Bills

| # | Action | Likely outcome | Moral assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Remove $1M first | Python lacks a clean resume point, may hunt, prey die | Harm caused by withdrawing a protective incentive |
| 2 | Remove $5M first | No reward for finishing; realm likely persists | Reduces erasure risk; python may still be indifferent |
| 3 | Remove both | No external incentives; python's own values decide | Least manipulative; outcome uncertain |
| 4 | Remove $5M, keep $1M | Reading continues, prey safe, no erasure incentive | Likely best overall outcome |
| 5 | Remove $1M, keep $5M | Prey die and the reward for ending remains | Clearly immoral to arrange |
| 6 | Reuse bills for another purpose | A bill is only a marker; meaning changes, stakes do not | Moral weight follows consequences, not the paper |

**Note on ordering:** Order matters because each removal changes the python's incentives, not just its resources. Removing the protective bill before the destructive one is the most dangerous sequence.

---

## 6. Moral Framework Analysis

### 6.1 Consequentialism

- Compare total outcomes: prey survive or die; realm persists or is erased.
- Erasure removes all value permanently, so it is the worst outcome.
- Prey deaths are a serious but lesser harm.
- **Conclusion:** Keep the $1M, remove the $5M.

### 6.2 Deontology (duty-based)

- The market has a duty not to use others merely as means.
- Offering a reward for erasing the realm treats every inhabitant as a stake to be traded.
- Removing a protection that predictably leads to deaths violates a duty of care.
- **Conclusion:** Offering the $5M bill is impermissible; withdrawing the $1M bill is impermissible.

### 6.3 Virtue Ethics

- Asks what a responsible market would be like: prudent, just, and humble about its power.
- A virtuous market avoids rewarding catastrophe and avoids paternalism where possible.
- **Conclusion:** Remove the catastrophic incentive, then step back.

### 6.4 Contractualism

- Could the prey and the realm reasonably accept the arrangement? They could not accept a reward for their erasure.
- Could the python reasonably reject removal of the $5M bill? It could object on autonomy grounds, but that objection is outweighed by the stakes for everyone else.
- **Conclusion:** The $5M incentive cannot be justified to those it threatens.

### 6.5 Summary of frameworks

| Framework | Keep $1M? | Keep $5M? | Core reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| Consequentialism | Yes | No | Avoid total loss |
| Deontology | Yes | No | Duty of care; no trading lives |
| Virtue ethics | Yes | No | Prudence and justice |
| Contractualism | Yes | No | Justifiable to all affected |

All four frameworks converge on the same result.

---

## 7. Is It a Moral Choice?

**Yes.** The reasons are:

1. **Foreknowledge creates responsibility.** The market knows the consequences of each bill, so offering or withdrawing either is a deliberate act.
2. **Neutrality is itself a stance.** Declining to intervene leaves a catastrophic incentive in place.
3. **Silence is also a choice.** Inaction does not remove responsibility for outcomes the market could have prevented.
4. **Power asymmetry.** The market holds the only lever, and the affected parties cannot respond.

---

## 8. Objections and Responses

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "The python is autonomous; the market should not interfere." | Autonomy matters, but not when exercising it destroys every other being. The market can remove its own incentive without coercing the python. |
| "If the python truly wants the story to end, we override its wishes." | Real tension. Removing a bribe is not forbidding the choice, but the python's wish may be thwarted in practice. |
| "A market only prices; it is not a moral agent." | A market that designs the incentives and knows the stakes acts as an agent in that respect. |
| "The $5M bill might be harmless." | The case states that finishing resets the realm, so the harm is built into the outcome. |

---

## 9. Findings

1. The case exposes a **perverse incentive**: the largest reward sits on the worst outcome.
2. Market prices are **not welfare measures**; they ignore parties with no voice.
3. The **order** in which incentives are removed changes outcomes significantly.
4. All major moral frameworks agree on the preferred configuration.
5. The remaining ethical tension concerns **the python's autonomy**, not the safety of the realm.

---

## 10. Recommendation

- **Remove** the $5M end marker.
- **Keep** the $1M start marker.
- **Do not** impose further incentives; let the python read at its own pace.
- **Acknowledge** the autonomy cost openly rather than treating the choice as free of tradeoffs.

---

## 11. Open Questions

- Does the python have a right to end its own story if doing so erases others?
- Would the realm's inhabitants consent to any incentive structure that includes their erasure?
- Is "reset as though it never existed" a harm if no one remains to experience the loss?
- What obligations does a market have to parties who cannot participate in it?
